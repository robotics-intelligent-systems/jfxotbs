# Peru RPAS Pilot Accreditation — Source Translation and Practice Quiz

## Status and provenance

English translation and structured migration of `docs/En el Perú la regulación.txt` at source commit `a59942030e2aa26418a5056241627e6e59a66e1e`, processed on 7 October 2026. The original Spanish text remains in Git history. All six questions, 24 choices, six supplied answers and four requirement statements are preserved below.

This is an unofficial study draft, not a DGAC/FAP examination, an operational checklist or evidence of an implemented licensing module. “Accreditation,” “pilot licence,” aircraft registration and operational authorization should not be treated as interchangeable. Sections labelled **Source translation** reproduce the source's claims; the editorial review at the end identifies limitations separately.

## Source translation: regulatory context

In Peru, regulation and issuance of RPAS/drone (Remotely Piloted Aircraft Systems) accreditations are handled by the DGAC (Directorate General of Civil Aeronautics of the Ministry of Transport and Communications, MTC) under NTC 001-2015 / NTC 001-2023, while the FAP (Peruvian Air Force) manages military airspace and restricted zones.

The source introduces the following simulated test as containing key DGAC/FAP questions for theoretical RPAS pilot accreditation. That attribution is inherited from the source and has not been authenticated.

## Source translation: theoretical practice quiz

### 1. Regulatory authority

Which body regulates, supervises and oversees the use of remotely piloted aircraft (RPA) in Peruvian territory?

- A) Peruvian Air Force (FAP).
- B) Directorate General of Civil Aeronautics (DGAC / MTC).
- C) Ministry of Defence.
- D) International Civil Aviation Organization (ICAO).

### 2. Responsibility for flight safety

Who has ultimate responsibility for flight safety and for taking measures to avoid collisions during the operation?

- A) The visual observer.
- B) The drone owner.
- C) The pilot in command.
- D) The DGAC.

### 3. Visual line of sight

What is visual line of sight (VLOS)?

- A) Keeping the aircraft in view through the remote-control camera (FPV).
- B) An operation in which the pilot maintains direct visual contact without optical aids, except corrective lenses.
- C) Flying with a programmed autopilot system.
- D) None of the above.

### 4. Restricted area

What is a “restricted area”?

- A) Airspace in which all aircraft flights are completely prohibited.
- B) Airspace of defined dimensions over the territory or territorial waters in which flight is subject to specific conditions.
- C) An urban area with a high density of people.
- D) Any protected natural area.

### 5. Loss of visual contact

What should be done immediately if visual contact with the aircraft is lost during flight?

- A) Maintain altitude and wait for the battery to run out.
- B) Move the aircraft to an altitude/position where visual contact can be regained, or activate the return-to-home (RTH) procedure in a safe area.
- C) Continue the mission using telemetry alone.
- D) Increase horizontal speed.

**Editorial safety note:** Choice B is the source's supplied answer, not a universal recovery procedure. An automatic climb or RTH may encounter obstacles or airspace limits. The applicable aircraft manual, preplanned contingency procedure and actual conditions must govern the response.

### 6. Aeronautical Information Publication

What is an AIP (Aeronautical Information Publication)?

- A) A daily weather report.
- B) A publication issued by the aeronautical authority containing aeronautical information of a permanent nature for navigation.
- C) The manufacturer's maintenance manual.
- D) A driving licence issued by the MTC.

## Source translation: principal accreditation requirements

These are the source's four statements, not a verified application checklist.

| Topic | Translated source statement |
| --- | --- |
| Training accreditation | Certificate of a theoretical/practical course issued by a CIAC (Civil Aviation Training Centre) or an authorized entity. |
| Theoretical examination | Take and pass the DGAC examination with a minimum score of 75%, or 15/20. |
| Medical evaluation | Medical fitness certificate, Class 3 or as applicable to aeronautical personnel. |
| RPA registration | Register the aircraft with the DGAC (aircraft registration card / registration). |

## Editorial review and official references

Review date: 7 October 2026. This is a limited source check, not a comprehensive determination of the rules applicable to a particular flight.

- The [MTC RPAS accreditation information page](https://www.gob.pe/institucion/mtc/informes-publicaciones/344331-requisitos-para-las-operaciones-de-sistemas-de-aeronaves-pilotadas-a-distancia) identifies a transitional accreditation procedure and a 75% examination threshold. Its indexed text was available during review; direct page retrieval failed. Consult the live page and attached circulars for the current procedure, forms and examination bank.
- [Supreme Decree No. 012-2026-MTC](https://busquedas.elperuano.pe/dispositivo/NL/2520332-3), published on 29 May 2026, approves the regulation of Law No. 30740. The source's two NTC references alone therefore do not constitute a complete regulatory review. The decree distinguishes operational categories and authorization concepts; applicability and transitional provisions must be checked before relying on a requirement.
- The source's reference to **NTC 001-2023**, its general attribution of restricted zones to the FAP, its mandatory **Class 3 medical certificate** claim and the exact training-provider requirement were not validated in this migration. They are retained for traceability, not endorsed.
- Aircraft registration concerns the aircraft and should be tracked separately from pilot accreditation and permission for a specific operation. The source's “registration card / registration” wording does not establish equivalence between all documents or processes.
- The six-item answer key was preserved, not certified against the official question bank. This note does not establish readiness for an examination or authorize flight.
